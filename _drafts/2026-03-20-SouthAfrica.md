It is 2200, 22nd November 2025, and I am looking at a sheet covered in singing cicadas. Above me, Orion stands on his head. It is Hilary Term 2022, and I am walking in University Parks with a South African DPhil student. He is interested in wildlife and evo-devo, like me. It is 1900, 20th November 2025, and I am stood in a dusty road outside Polokwane. A Rufous-cheeked nightjar has started to sing. It is 1700, 22nd November 2023, and I am looking at a Bittern. Mayur cannot see it, and says UK birding is shite. It is 9th December 2025, and I am looking at a Chaffinch. I have now seen 467 species of bird in South Africa.

South Africa
---

During my five years at Oxford it was a pleasure to get to know Cape Town resident Mayur Prag. He is a man of eclectic interests and talents: he is a [tour leader for Birding Africa](https://birdingafrica.com/blog/personnel/mayur-prag/), [botanical influencer](https://www.instagram.com/thecapeflora/), and knowledgeable about birds, plants, insects, and anything else that crawls, swims, or flies in front of his camera. Towards the end of our time at Oxford Mayur suggested that me and our mutual friend [Thomas Miller](https://tmbirding.blogspot.com/) (Port Meadow list 166) should come down to visit him in South Africa.

The trip lasted three weeks during November-December of 2025 and felt like a real milestone, getting to tick off a bunch of bucket-list wildlife things like Leopards, African Painted Dogs, and tusker Elephants. Mayur planned out a route for us that started in Johannesburg, up through the dry highveld to Limpopo province, where we met lush afromontane forests. Then from there down through the vast wilderness of Kruger national park, and across to the imposing Drakensberg mountains around Lesotho. Finally, we flew across to Cape Town, and walked through the fynbos. 

Because I lack a camera suitable for photographing any organism further than 5cm away, I will leave the descriptions of the birding and mammal watching to Mayur and Thomas, who both took enough photographs for the both of us. Thomas is writing [a detailed account of the trip](https://tmbirding.blogspot.com/2026/03/south-africa-introduction-and-itinerary.html) on his blog.
